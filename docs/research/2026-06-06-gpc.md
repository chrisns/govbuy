# Government Procurement Card (GPC) — working brief

> Research workflow run 2026-06-06 (`gpc-route-research`, 3 finders + synthesis). Primary source:
> *Procurement Cards: Pan-Government Policy v4.0*, 6 Apr 2020. Web-sourced June 2026; some figures
> predate PA2023 and the March 2025 crackdown — see staleness flags.

## Verdict: payment mechanism, not a route

Unambiguous. Policy: cards are "a method of prompt payment, not as a credit card" and "complement
local purchasing… not replace" them (v4.0 §1.2). The card sets *how* you pay, not *whether/from whom*
you may buy; the underlying purchase must independently be compliant ("Contracted suppliers must be
used whenever possible", §4.1–4.2). The **March 2025 Cabinet Office crackdown** hardened this:
**banned where a departmental or cross-government procurement route exists.**

> **Rule govbuy applies:** the GPC is how a purchase is *paid for*, never how it is *routed* —
> classify it as a settlement mechanism, never a procurement route/framework/award method, and never
> present it as an alternative to one.

**Corollary:** PA2023 is **payment-method-blind** — the regulated below-threshold regime triggers on
contract *value* (≥£12k inc VAT central gov incl. NHS trusts; ≥£30k other authorities, ss.84–86), not
on how you pay. A GPC purchase above that floor carries the same notice/transparency duties as any
other purchase of that value.

**Watch-out:** "£500 single-transaction limit from April 2025" seen in some summaries **conflates** the
£500 hospitality cap / £500 publication threshold with a transaction limit. The formal key-holder
single-transaction limit remained **£20,000** (PPN 03/20). Some departments set local £500 limits
(e.g. Forestry Commission) — local policy, not a national cap.

## How GPC should appear in "how do I buy an AI product?"

**Default: the card is rarely the right route for AI — a narrow exception, not a starting point.**

**Acceptable when ALL hold:** genuinely low-value one-off/ad-hoc (single SaaS seat, API credits,
app-store sub), below local card limits *and* below the PA2023 floor (£12k central / £30k wider);
**no suitable framework/call-off exists**; **no government data risk** (AI Playbook: "must not enter
official information unless… cleared for publication"; consumer tools may train on prompts);
cardholder holds DFA + merchant category open.

**WRONG (route via a framework instead) when any apply:**
- A **compliant route exists** — and for AI/cloud it almost always does: **AI agreement RM6200**,
  **G-Cloud 14 (RM1557.14)** (GC15 ~Sep 2026), **Back Office Software 2 (RM6285)** for SaaS, **Cloud
  Compute 2 (RM6292)** for PaaS/IaaS, Big Data & Analytics RM6195, SPARK RM6094. Cloud First +
  Technology Code of Practice + the March 2025 ban all steer here.
- **Government/official data** processed — needs enterprise DPA, no-training guarantees, security
  schedule, audit rights; a card checkout negotiates none of these.
- Whole-life/aggregate spend crosses a **Cabinet Office digital & technology spend control**:
  cryptography **£0** (any value, NCSC), public/citizen-facing digital **£100k**, all other
  digital/data/technology **£1m**, plus **anything novel or contentious at any value**. No
  salami-slicing onto a card to evade these.
- Need is **recurring, enterprise-scale, or needs negotiated/multi-year terms**. PPN 02/24 (AI
  procurement transparency) + the AI Playbook expect commercial involvement from the start.

> **One-liner for the AI buying flow:** "Yes, you can technically put a small, data-safe AI
> subscription on a GPC — but if a framework (RM6200, G-Cloud) covers it, or any government data is
> involved, or whole-life cost approaches a spend-control threshold, the card is non-compliant and you
> must route through the framework."

Sources: Pan-Gov Policy v4.0; AI Playbook for the UK Government; gca.gov.uk AI agreement page;
gov.uk Cloud First; Digital & Technology Spend Control v6; PA2023 thresholds (SI 2025/1200).

## Staleness flags (for the harness to re-check)

1. **No PA2023-era refresh of the pan-gov GPC policy found** — authoritative version is still v4.0
   (April 2020), framed against the old PCR regime. Treat its limit figures / legal cross-refs as
   possibly superseded by March 2025 + local policy.
2. **March 2025 freeze not yet formalised** into a standing PPN / updated pan-gov policy on a primary
   source — confirm whether the £20k/£100k limits, ≥50% cut and £500 hospitality cap are codified.
3. **GCA rename (1 Apr 2026):** confirm whether RM6248 is re-badged/extended beyond 31/10/2026 or
   replaced by RM6383, and whether the issuer list changed.
4. **Publication cadence:** "two months in arrears" vs "monthly in arrears" — verify current rule.
5. **No published statistic** for GPC spend share on software/SaaS/AI (~41% is travel/accommodation
   per NAO); SaaS-on-card prevalence is inferred from transparency data, not published.
